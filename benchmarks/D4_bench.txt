In modern computer architecture, Non-Uniform Memory Access (NUMA) and peripheral component interconnect buses such as PCIe 4.0 play a critical role in determining the throughput of large-scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models. When a transformer layer routes each token to a sparse subset of experts—for instance, selecting 6 active experts out of 256 total experts per layer—the arithmetic intensity during single-token autoregressive decoding is close to one FLOP per byte of weight read. Consequently, execution time is dominated by memory bandwidth rather than floating-point compute capacity. On a workstation equipped with 24 GiB of high-bandwidth GDDR6X video memory (936 GB/s) and 64 GiB of system DDR4 memory connected over a 16-lane PCIe 4.0 bus (31.5 GB/s raw, approximately 26 to 28 GB/s effective DMA payload throughput), caching the static attention projections, shared experts, and hot routed experts in VRAM while keeping the remaining routed experts resident in the operating system page cache avoids costly NVMe solid-state drive reads. Furthermore, overlapping asynchronous host-to-device direct memory access transfers on a dedicated CUDA stream with shared expert matrix multiplications on the compute stream hides a substantial fraction of the PCIe transfer latency.
